# NOSTALGIA, Pitch C (Ensemble): *Frame 36*

*Thoda left... thoda right... smile please!*

**Logline.** Summer 2003, Lakshmi Nagar Colony, Himayatnagar. On the last day of school, 11-year-old Lucky's six cracks the colony's only group photo, taken at Charminar in 1983, just as Mehdi uncle says his Irani cafe closes on 15 September. Lucky promises a remake with his father's "borrowed" camera, then finds that every grown-up in the frame has a grudge and that the man who took the 1983 photo has never been in one since.

**Why *Nostalgia*.**
- *The essay:* Vimala Ma'am's holiday homework, *Meri Yaadgaar Chhuttiyan* (My Memorable Holidays). Each MISSION PASSED shows Lucky's tall-tale paragraph and her red ink: *"Bus udti nahi. Sach likho."* (Buses don't fly. Write the truth.)
- *The camera:* one roll; frame 36 is saved for Charminar.
- *The word:* Rocky's Spoken English word of the day, NOSTALGIA, which everyone defines wrong until the last card.

**Calendar (2003).** Last bell Thu 24 Apr; wedding Sun 18 May; school Thu 12 Jun; Lashkar Bonalu in July; Prasads opens Fri 25 Jul (NTR Gardens open since 2001); Ganesh Chaturthi Sun 31 Aug; **the photo: Sun 14 Sep, 5:40 pm**, twenty years after Sun 18 Sep 1983. No mobiles, metro or malls.

## Cast

- **Lucky (K. Lakshman Kumar)**, 11, the player: hand-me-down cricket T-shirt, odd hawai chappals, Nanna's camera on a shoelace. "Pakka plan hai!" Every plan fails. *Wants* to be the colony hero; learns to put others in the frame.
- **Ayesha "Bowling" Fatima**, 12, next door: plaits under a cricket cap. "Hau, ho jaata." *Wants* to bowl in a real match before her family moves to Tolichowki (8 km away; it feels like America).
- **Kishan "Calculator" Agarwal**, 10, the kirana's son: round, calculator watch. "Special offer, ek rupaiya!" *Wants* ₹350 for a 9999-in-1 TV game.
- **Chitti (K. Lalitha)**, 6, Lucky's sister: antenna ponytails. "Mummy ki cheppesta!" (I'm telling Mummy!) *Wants* to stop being *doodh-bhaat*.
- **Rocky anna (K. Ramakrishna)**, 25, cousin: medical rep, black RX100, aviators, big English words used wrongly. *Wants* a filmy wedding.
- **Mummy (K. Sharada)**, 37: jasmine, a chappal already in hand; uses your full name. *Wants* one photo with Lucky's shirt tucked in. Meter level 5.
- **Nanna (K. Sreenivas)**, 41, "current office" engineer, blamed for every power cut. "Chuddam." (We'll see.) *Wants* everyone in focus. He took every colony photo since 1983; he's in none.
- **Mehdi uncle (Agha Mehdi)**, 74, Hotel Sarvar (`irani_cafe_7`): white cap, cardigan in May. "Do chai, ek plate Osmania!" *Wants* one last photo of everyone before he moves to Pune.
- **Vimala Ma'am**, 55, Hindi teacher at `school_1`: starched saree, red pen uncapped. "Sach likho!" *Wants* one true sentence from one student.
- **Cable Babji**, 33, cable-wala and wedding videographer, camcorder bigger than his head. "Signal aara na? Paisa do!" *Wants* to direct a real film.
- *Also:* **Agarwal-ji** (Gopal, kirana) and **Ramulu** (girni, flour-white), 45, best friends in 1983, silent since; **Sravani vadina**, Rocky's bride; **Tiger**, 13, Ashok Nagar's captain.

## How it plays

- **Moving:** walk, rent or "borrow" cycles and Lunas (+1 meter), ride Rocky's pillion, autos, red buses and Lumbini boats. STD booths save.
- **Chappal Meter:** 1, Chitti tattles; 2, the colony uncles (Agarwal-ji and Ramulu collide); 3, the havaldar's whistle; 4, his Luna; 5, MUMMY on the Chetak with a homing chappal. *Caught:* "PAKDA GAYA!", ₹2 off pocket money, and a red-ink note in the essay. *Cool down:* Irani chai and Osmania biscuits in a cafe's family room, the barber's chair, or home before the street lights.
- **Respect zones:** temples, mosques, churches and processions: walk only, footwear (and camera, where required) at the stand, meter frozen.
- **Hints:** the *Aunty Intelligence Network* solves any clue but adds a meter level (Mummy hears in ten minutes). Mehdi uncle explains over a ₹1 chai.
- **Frames (35):** photo riddles, blank until developed at `photo_studio_1` ("ready day after tomorrow"), each unlocking a trivia card: "The fair that sleeps eleven months, its giant wheel under a tarpaulin" (`nampally_exhibition`).
- **1983 Faces (12):** pencilled names on the photo's back, scattered across the city. Every Face found stands in the final photo; a missed one is a pencil outline.
- **Side activities:** beat Tiger at `video_parlour_1`.

## Key scenes

**Opening.** Grown-up hands open a brown-paper notebook labelled *K. LAKSHMAN KUMAR, VI-B*. A photo slides out face down, showing only its pencilled back. The ruled lines dissolve into VHS hiss: `▶ PLAY THU 24 APR 2003 15:25`. *Tan-tan-tan*: the last bell of the year.

**Midpoint (Wed 11 Jun, the night before school).** Covering new textbooks in brown paper, Lucky finds a fresh film roll in his bag: *"Save the last one. —S."* Same handwriting as the 1983 photo. The power goes; Nanna rides off to fix it. By lamplight, Mummy opens twenty years of albums: "Find your Nanna." He is in one, their wedding, because a studio was paid. In 1983 he is a shadow in the corner. The objective rewrites itself: **EVERYONE (INCLUDING NANNA)**.

**Climax.** 5:36 pm, Charminar the colour of an Osmania biscuit. Nanna is up a pole at Pathergatti, Lucky holding the torch below. The feeder clicks on, Laad Bazaar's bangles blaze, and the shopkeepers cheer the current-office man. Lucky props the camera on Karachi Bakery tins, sets ten seconds and turns. Nanna, from twenty years' habit, steps back to check the focus. "NANNA, NUVVU KOODA!" Lucky drags him in. Mummy, seeing her son sprint past the camera, throws. *Beep-beep-beep. Click.*

**Ending (at Charminar).** Twenty-three chais at the cafe facing Charminar, paid with Kishan's TV-game money. Mehdi uncle hands Lucky the cracked 1983 frame: "Ab yeh tumhare hawale." Charminar's lights come on. Lucky writes one line: *"Sach: maine kuch bada nahi kiya. Bas sabko ek photo mein laaya. Nanna ko bhi."* (Truth: I did nothing big. I got everyone into one photo. Nanna too.) Red pen: *"10/10. Pehli baar sach."* (The truth, for the first time.)

The print, date-stamped `'03 9 14` in orange: Lucky a blur (nobody can tell if his shirt was tucked in; Mummy frames it anyway), Nanna laughing mid-stride, a chappal frozen above everyone, Agarwal-ji in front at last, Ma'am laughing as in 1983.

**2026, the same spot.** Grown-up hands prop a camera on a biscuit tin. In the middle, an empty plastic chair wears a white cap. Ayesha came from Tolichowki: "Eight kilometres. We cried for a week." Nanna, 64, steps back to check the focus. "Nanna, nuvvu kooda!" He runs; his knees don't. They make it. Mostly. Pigeons lift off the minarets. ■ STOP.

Lakshman Kumar, VI-B, age 34)*.

## Glossary

(Te) = Telugu; the rest is Dakhni or Hindi. **aage / peeche** in front / behind; **aara** coming; **Atta, Pinni, Peddamma** (Te) aunts; **baraat** groom's procession; **bonam** (Te) Bonalu offering pot; **chanda** festival donation; **current poyindi** (Te) the power's gone; **doodh-bhaat** the little kid who "doesn't count"; **emaindi** (Te) what happened; **hallu** gently; **hau** yes; **hawale** in the care of; **-ich** emphatic suffix (*mainich thi*, it was me); **khaata** credit book; **kosaru** (Te) a vendor's free extra; **kumkum** red powder; **main hasi** I laughed; **mali** gardener; **moora** (Te) a forearm's length; **pakka** sure; **pillalu** (Te) kids; **Pothuraju** Bonalu's guardian dancer; **Rahu kalam** a daily inauspicious period; **sach** truth; **teenmaar** (Te) a drumbeat; **thoda** a little; **utro** get off.
