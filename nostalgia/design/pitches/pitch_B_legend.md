# NOSTALGIA, Pitch B (Legend): *The Fifth Minar*

**Logline.** On Sankranti 2003 a runaway kite lands on the balcony of Chintu's Thatha, a retired conductor starting to forget things. He gives the 11-year-old a locked 1968 tin, half a map and four riddles about the Charminar–Golconda tunnel and its "fifth minar". Chased by a cable-TV treasure hunter, Chintu's gang uncovers a 35-year quarrel between two best friends over one kite, and the fifth minar appears only if the gang reunites them at Charminar on Chand Raat.

**Frame (why it's *Nostalgia*).** Years later, in an emptied colony cable office, a VHS tape: *"KHAZANA HUNT with PROF. PASHA, 2003. FINAL EPISODE: THE FIFTH MINAR. DO NOT TAPE OVER."* PLAY. The HUD's `▶ PLAY TUE 14 JAN 2003` is that tape.
Thatha's map has one rule, *"If I forget, ask the city"*: every clue is something the city remembered for him, and every collectible is a memory that makes the finale taller.

**Calendar (2003).** Sankranti (Tue 14 Jan), Numaish, World Cup final (Sun 23 Mar), Bonalu (July), Prasads' opening (Fri 25 Jul), Ganesh immersion (September), and Chand Raat (Tue 25 Nov). The kids' pockets stay 90s: Walkman, trump cards, Tinkle.

## Characters

- **Chintu (Sai Kiran)**, 11, the player. *Look:* oversized school shirt, hawai chappals, a BSA Champ. *Voice:* Telugu-Dakhni, "Kasam se!" *Wants* to open the tin before Thatha forgets why it matters.
- **Zoya**, 11, the brains. *Look:* plaits, specs, a slam-book case diary; her Ammi codes in Cyber Towers. *Voice:* "Elementary, mamu. Nakko panic." *Wants* to be Hyderabad's first lady detective.
- **Laddu (Mohan Reddy)**, 10, the pindi girni's son. *Look:* floured, tiffin in hand. *Voice:* "Aakali ra!" *Wants* to beat the colony record of 47 pani puris at Gokul Chat.
- **Tony D'Souza**, 12, Secunderabad railway colony. *Look:* Walkman, 2-in-1. *Voice:* "Chill, men!" *Wants* to record everything; his family emigrates to Australia after Eid.
- **Venkat Rao, "Ticket Thatha"**, 63, route 8A conductor for 31 years. *Look:* faded khaki, ticket punch on his shoulder. *Voice:* soft Telugu, "Right, right!"; he forgets days, never bus stops. *Wants* to say one sentence to one man.
- **Yusuf Patangwale**, 64, Zoya's Nana, kite maker of Gulzar Houz. *Look:* henna-orange beard; never looks at the sky. *Voice:* Dakhni couplets. *Wants*, he claims, never to hear Venkat's name again.
- **Professor Pasha**, about 45, self-certified "Treasure Scientist" hosting *Khazana Hunt* on colony cable. *Look:* safari suit, pith helmet, yellow Lambretta, a TV-antenna metal detector; nephew Babloo (14) secretly roots for the kids. *Voice:* booming Dakhni at an invisible camera. *Wants* the Qutb Shahi treasure, found live on air.
- **Mummy (Sujatha)**, 37. *Look:* nightie, dupatta, jasmine, chappal within reach. *Voice:* your full name, when it's serious. *Wants* you home before the street lights come on.

## How it plays

**Getting around.** Walk, cycle, borrow Lunas and Chetaks, ride autos and buses.

**Chappal Meter.** Colony uncles (who report to Mummy), then the watchman or PT sir, a havaldar's whistle, a havaldar on a Luna, and finally MUMMY on Abba's Chetak. It cools in an Irani café's family room, a barber's chair, or at home before the street lights.

**Respect zones.** Temples, mosques, churches, the Qutb Shahi Tombs, Charminar's upper floors and processions: walk only, chappals at the stand, meter frozen, pursuers grumbling outside.

**Hints.** "Ask the city": the third local you ask gives the answer.

## Hidden things

**Yusuf's Lost Kites (34).** Every Sankranti from 1969 to 2002, Yusuf cut loose a *sorry* kite towards Venkat's colony, and the city kept them.
- *Where:* neem trees, a Tank Bund lamp, an LB Stadium floodlight, a Golconda bastion, the Qutb Shahi gardens, a crane by `cyber_towers`.
- *Finding them:* each kite's line hints at the next (*"1987: the wind turned at the red buses; the tamarind caught it."*); viewpoints reveal more (giant wheel, Birla parapet, Bala Hissar, Prasads, the closed `falaknuma_palace` gate).
- *Payoff:* every kite found joins the finale's kite train: the fifth minar is as tall as your memory.

**Thatha's Tickets (24).** Punched 8A tickets hidden behind bus-stop boards.
- *Clue:* the punch hole marks a stage painted on a real stop (*"Stage 14: where the tower tells time on half its faces."*).
- *Reward:* a verified trivia card and a memory; in the finale Thatha recalls one more sentence per ticket.

**Case Tapes (`cassette_*`, 12).** Tony's recordings, for the `walkman`.

## Key scenes

**Opening.** Snehapuri Colony at dawn: bhogi smoke, muggulu on every step. Mid-pech, a red kite flying itself slices Chintu's line; he chases it over parapets until it flops onto Thatha's balcony. Twice this morning Thatha asked Chintu what class he was in. Now he reads the kite, goes very still, and opens the Godrej.

**Midpoint.** On World Cup final night, the colony hushed around every TV, the lock opens on 1-4-0-1. No gold: a 1968 photo of two grinning young men with a giant kite at Charminar, a cut green kite reading *"Shabana, nikah karogi? Yusuf"*, and an unpunched ticket, "2 passengers, Gulzar Houz to Charminar". Zoya recognises her Nana, then her Nani's name: "Your Thatha cut my Nana's PROPOSAL?" She walks out; Tony switches off the recorder. That Thursday, Thatha disappears.

**Climax.** Chand Raat: Laad Bazaar is a river of bangle-light and haleem steam under floodlit Charminar. Pasha brings a shovel to "open the tunnel"; the kids decoy him through the lanes until the meter hits five. Mummy roars up on the Chetak and throws the Chappal Bazar chappal, spinning the shovel into the Gulzar Houz fountain. At 11:58 the kids sprint to the four arches; Yusuf and Thatha walk in from opposite sides as the four clocks strike twelve.

**Ending.** Yusuf hands Venkat the charkhi, as in 1968. From his terrace a kite train climbs into the floodlight beside the four minars: every kite you found, the green 1968 one on top. A fifth minar of paper, swaying. Thatha: "Sorry, Yusuf. I forgot many things. Not this." Yusuf: "Baigan. You forgot to *say* it for thirty-five years." Thatha punches the old ticket and says, clear as the bell Birla Mandir never had: "Chintu. Right, right." Over "do chai, ek plate Osmania", the kids borrow Pasha's camera (Gemini TV just called him) to tape messages to their grown-up selves: *"Twenty years. Chand Raat. Same arches."*

`■ STOP`. In soft VHS blur, with no modern objects, four adult silhouettes walk into Charminar from the four arches, one in headphones, one round. "Do chai, ek plate Osmania?" "Gurthunda?" Laddu: "Gokul Chat first. Record is still 47."

## Glossary

(Te) Telugu, (Ur) Urdu, else Dakhni.

**Thatha/Ammamma** (Te) grandfather/grandmother; **Nana/Nani** (Ur) maternal grandparents; **bo kaata** "cut!"; **pech** kite fight; **saddi/charkhi** cotton string/spool; **muggulu** (Te) rangoli; **bhogi** Sankranti-eve bonfire; **mamu** buddy; **nakko/hau** no/yes; **baigan** "nonsense!"; **kasam se** I swear; **aakali ra** (Te) I'm hungry; **inkoka** (Te) one more; **maaf kar** forgive me; **men** Anglo-Indian "mate"; **pindi girni** flour mill; **maut ka kuan** motorbike "well of death"; **taali** clap; **paanchwa** fifth; **khazana** treasure; **Chand Raat** eve of Eid; **bonam** (Te) Bonalu pot; **nimajjanam** (Te) immersion; **gurthunda?** (Te) do you remember?; **do chai, ek plate Osmania** two chais, a plate of Osmania biscuits.
