# Research: Growing Up in 1990s Hyderabad

Reference for story, dialogue, collectibles and ambient detail in *Nostalgia*.

**Legend.** **[V]** means the fact was checked against a web source during this research (sources are at the end). **[~]** marks an approximate value: a price, time slot or detail from common memory, given as a range. Use it freely, but don't put it on a "Did you know?" trivia card. **(unverified)** means the item is plausible but unconfirmed, so keep it as background flavour only.

**Content rules (from ARCHITECTURE.md §2):** brands, films and TV shows may be *mentioned*. Don't name living celebrities as characters; refer to them obliquely ("the Megastar", "the Little Master", "our city's own captain"). No weapons, gore or tobacco play.

## 0. Sensitivities and anachronisms (read first)

- **Avoid:** the 1990–92 old-city curfews and communal tension; party politics; the 2000 cricket match-fixing saga; the 1990 Buddha-statue accident (lives were lost, so say only "the statue went into the lake and was raised again"); glass-coated "Chinese manja", which is banned and dangerous. In the game, kite string is *saddi/cotton manja* and cut kites simply drift away.
- **Phantom "candy cigarettes":** show them only as a wrapper collectible or a sweet. No smoking mime. Paan shops sell *meetha paan* and toffees; leave tobacco out.
- **Not in the 1990s (don't place these):** the Metro, Shamshabad airport (2008), the ORR, the PVNR Expressway, Prasads IMAX (2003), malls such as Hyderabad Central (2004), Eat Street, MMTS trains (2003), Bajaj Pulsar (2001), Pokémon Tazos, Telangana state (2014; in the 90s it was **Andhra Pradesh**, with number plates starting **AP**), a US consulate in Hyderabad (2008; visa queues were in **Chennai**, unverified).
- **OK for the 90s:** Begumpet airport; Cyber Towers (Nov 1998) [V]; Lumbini Park (1994) [V]; the Hussain Sagar Buddha (installed 1992) [V]; Ramoji Film City (1996) [V]; Tank Bund statues; Birla Mandir; Birla Planetarium; the Nehru Zoo; Salar Jung Museum.

**Story hooks from the timeline**
- **1999 is the perfect setting year.** Y2K fear, Cyber Towers newly opened, and the elder cousin about to leave for America. Kids sense their city is changing, and a fitting game ending is the kid realising the memories are what stay. The default HUD date is THU 14 JAN 1999, which is Sankranti.
- **Collectible series ideas:** "DD Sunday" cards, "Sharjah '98" scorecard scraps, "Gemini/ETV launch" handbills, "Shaktimaan Sunday" (kids sprinting home before 12), and VHS/VCD labels for cult films (title only, no logos).

**Pocket money and coins**
- **Coins:** *chavanni* (25 p) and *athanni* (50 p) were common until c. 1998. ₹1 and ₹2 coins were everywhere, and the ₹5 coin was newer. 10 p and 20 p coins were rare by the mid-90s.
- **Weekly pocket money:** ₹2–5 early in the decade and ₹10–20 late (unverified, varies by family).
- **Windfalls:** *Eidi*, Diwali money from relatives, the "Dubai mamu" or "America uncle" pressing a ₹50 note into your hand, and selling old newspapers to the *raddi-wala*.
- **Other kid prices:** a game at the video parlour ₹1–2; cycle rental ₹1–2 an hour; a second-hand Tinkle at the Abids Sunday book market ₹2–5; a local coin-box call ₹1.

## 3. Games, toys and collectibles

**Street games** (Dakhni / Telugu names)
- **Gilli-danda** (Telugu *gooti-billa*, unverified): the stick flips the bail; risky near windows.
- **Goli / kanche** (marbles; Telugu *goleelu*): dig a pit (*gucchi*), flick with a bent finger. "Kanche" and "goli" cover glass marbles of every kind. There is a "jackpot" marble with a coloured swirl.
- **Lattu / bongaram** (spinning top): wind the cord, throw it. Variants include "spin it on your palm", spearing an opponent's top, and the *kaal* (iron nail tip).
- **Pittu / lagori** (Telugu *yedu penkulata*, "seven-stones game"): knock down the stack, then rebuild it while dodging the ball.
- **Chedugudu** (kabaddi), **kho-kho**, **dagudu moothalu** (hide and seek), **nela-banda** (tag where you're safe off the ground), **kothi-kommachi** (tree-climbing tag), **chemma chekka** (clapping rhyme), **ashta chamma** (cowrie-shell board game), **vaikunthapali** (snakes and ladders), **Raja-Rani-Chor-Sipahi** (paper chits), **Name-Place-Animal-Thing**, **book cricket** (flip a page; the last digit of the page number is your runs).
- **Tyre-and-stick racing**, kite chasing (*patang lootna*), and spying games ("CID" after the TV show).

**Gully cricket rules** (great as mini-game modifiers)
- "**One tip one hand**": a one-bounce catch, taken one-handed, is out.
- "**Last man batting**": the last batter plays alone, no runner.
- "**Direct out**" if the ball goes into a house or over the wall, and the batter has to fetch it.
- "**Owner of the bat bats first**", and doesn't get out first ball ("trial ball").
- **No LBW.** A **wall, crate or brick pile is the wicket.** **One-side batting** in narrow lanes, with off-side only and leg-side out.
- Tennis ball or rubber ball, with "**underarm/overarm**" agreed before play. "**Bowler's end umpire** is from the batting side", so there are endless arguments.
- "Dabba" means getting out for zero. "**Rain stopped play**" is called whenever an aunty shouts.

**Toys and collectibles**
- **WWF trump cards** (compare height and weight to win cards), cricket trump cards, and Big Fun gum cricket cards.
- **Tazos** (late 90s) and sticker albums (cricket, cartoons).
- **Brick game** ("9999 in 1" Tetris handheld), the **"TV game" famiclone** with cartridges such as "Contra", "Mario", "Duck Hunt", "Tank 1990" and "Road Fighter", and "999-in-1" multicarts with mostly fake titles.
- **Video game parlours** (pay per game or per 15 minutes): arcade cabinets and famiclones; parents disapprove.
- **Comics and magazines:** **Tinkle** (Suppandi, Shikari Shambu, Kalia), **Chandamama** (English and Telugu; Vikram-Betaal), **Champak**, **Amar Chitra Katha**, **Balamitra** (Telugu), Raj Comics and Diamond comics (Hindi), and a stack of old Indrajal *Phantom* comics at the raddi shop.
- **School kit:** the magnetic "double-decker" pencil box with buttons, the Camlin geometry box (compass, protractor, the useless set-squares), Natraj/Apsara pencils, ink pens with a Chelpark/Camlin ink bottle, a Reynolds pen by the late 90s, brown-paper book covers and name labels.
- **Slam books** (late 90s): "Your crush? (optional)", "Fav hero", "Fav colour", "Memorable moment".
- **Other fads:** Casio calculator watches, the Walkman, yo-yos, Tamagotchi (1997), and *fluorescent* bouncy balls.
- **Cycles:** **BSA SLR**, **Hero Ranger**, **Atlas Goldline**, BSA Champ (kids), and Hercules. A **ladies cycle** has a low bar, so kids learn on it. On an adult cycle, a kid learns **"kainchi"** (scissor) style, riding *through* the frame. **Rental cycles** are hired by the hour with the shop name painted on the frame; the puncture shop finds leaks in a tub of water. Cycle fittings include a bell, a carrier for the school bag, a dynamo light and a "pillion" on the carrier.

## 4. Transport

- **Auto-rickshaws:** black and yellow, two-stroke (smoke, rattle), with meters that are "always broken". The opening line is "**Meter pe chalega?**" and the haggle goes "Meter nakko, das rupaiya upar" (no meter, ₹10 over). Night fare is meter plus half (unverified local norm). **School autos** pack 8–12 kids with bags hanging outside. Fares are [~] ₹5 minimum early 90s, rising to ₹8–10 late 90s.
- **APSRTC city buses:** red and cream, numbered routes with letter suffixes (e.g. **8A, 8C** Secunderabad–Charminar [V]; 10H; 7Z from Afzalgunj [V]). There is a ladies' section, and the conductor has a leather bag, a ticket punch and a whistle. **Student bus passes** were monthly (unverified price), and the stops are known as "X Roads" (cross roads). Don't depict footboard travel as fun; make it a scold moment.
- **Two-wheelers:** **Bajaj Chetak** (tilt it to start, waiting lists; the family scooter with kid standing in front), **Kinetic Honda** (gearless), **TVS Luna** (pedal-start moped; the milkman's ride), TVS 50/Champ, the **Yamaha RX100** (cool cousin; that sound), Hero Honda CD100 / **Splendor** (1994), Rajdoot, Lambretta in the old city.
- **Cars:** **Ambassador** (government, with beacon; also taxis), **Premier Padmini**, **Maruti 800** (the middle-class dream), **Maruti Gypsy** (police patrols), Esteem/Zen (1993–94), white **Tata Sumo** convoys (late 90s), Contessa; Santro and Indica at the very end of the decade.
- **Others:** cycle-rickshaws in the old city, lorries painted "Horn OK Please", handcarts, bullock carts on the outskirts.
- **Traffic police:** white-and-khaki uniforms (unverified), a whistle, a hand signal from a painted podium at junctions, and "**Licence dikhao!**" Signals were often manual.
- **Road life:** **cows** sitting in the middle of the road, buffaloes in the old city (*gowli* dairies), pigeons at Mecca Masjid, stray dogs napping under cars, and roadside water pots.
- **Number plates:** **AP 09 / AP 10 / AP 11...** series (unverified which code is which district).

## 6. Language

Dakhni (Hyderabadi Urdu) features to imitate:
- **hau / nakko** for yes/no, and **kaiku** for why [V].
- The emphatic **-ich** suffix [V] (*abhich* = right now, *wahich* = that very one, *ekich* = just one).
- The plural **-aan** [V] (*busaan*, *baataan*).
- **ku** for *ko* (*mere ku*).
- Present tense in **-ra/-ri/-re** (*aara*, *jaari*, *kya karre*).

### 6c. Colony characters and how they talk

- **Kirana uncle:** sits behind glass candy jars, keeps a *khaata* (credit) book, and has a radio on for cricket. Hands out toffees as change. "Abba ku bolna, khaata full ho gaya." (Tell your dad, the account is full.) He mixes Hindi and Telugu.
- **Irani cafe waiter:** never writes orders down and shouts to the counter. "Do pauna, ek Osmania, ek bun-maska!" The cafe has marble-top tables, bentwood chairs, a big wall clock and a cashier at the *galla* (till). Signboards list cafe rules (unverified).
- **Auto driver:** "Kidhar jaana? Charminar? Udhar se khali aana padta, das upar." (Coming back empty, ₹10 extra.) He's a philosopher about traffic and knows every shortcut, which makes him a good clue-giver.
- **Traffic havaldar:** whistle, then "Ruko! Licence? Cycle pe teen log? Utro!" (Three on a cycle? Get off!) Gruff but soft on kids.
- **Cable TV operator ("cable-wala"):** climbs on terraces with wire coils. "Signal aara na? Is mahine ka paisa?" He runs a local channel with scrolling birthday wishes and ads.
- **Milkman (*doodhwala*):** cycle or Luna with aluminium cans, buffalo milk; or the dawn **Vijaya milk** packet booth. "Paani nakko milaaya, kasam se!" (I didn't add water, I swear!)
- **Paperboy:** cycles past and throws *Eenadu* or *Deccan Chronicle* onto the balcony, and *Siasat* in the old city. "Aaj ka taaza!"
- **Colony aunties:** gossip at the gate at 6 pm. "Mee abbayi EAMCET rank entha?" (What's your son's EAMCET rank?) They will report you to your mother within ten minutes.
- **The strict teacher:** "Silence! Stand up on the bench. Imposition, hundred times!" Chalk flies; a duster thumps the desk.
- **The cool elder cousin with the RX100:** shades, Walkman, a denim jacket in May. "Tension nakko, main hoon na." He's leaving for America after Y2K, which is the emotional thread of the story.
- **Dubai mamu / Gulf uncle:** brings Tang, Casio watches, attar and "imported" chocolates.
- **America uncle (H-1B):** says "gas station" and "Y2K project", and hands out M&Ms.
- **Night watchman ("Gurkha"):** whistle and stick-tap at night. A safe, comic pursuer.
- **Folk performers:** Gangireddu and Haridasu (Sankranti), Budabukkala fortune drummers, and the bear or monkey shows (dated; reference only).

## 7. Middle-class home and colony life

- **Architecture:** flat RCC roofs with **terraces** (the playground, kite arena and summer bedroom); black **Sintex water tanks**; **"motor on karo!"** to fill the overhead tank when municipal water comes (alternate days in summer); rabbit-ear or fishbone **TV antennas** that someone turns while another shouts "Aaya? Ab? Haan bas!"; dish antennas after 1992; clotheslines; grill gates; **cement benches** at the colony park; compound walls with painted names ("Sai Nilayam", "Lakshmi Nivas"); **Shahabad / Tandur stone** and red-oxide floors (unverified as universal); old-city *deorhis* with courtyards and arches.
- **Inside:** a Godrej almirah; the TV under a lace cover in a wooden shutter cabinet (Onida, BPL, Videocon, Solidaire); a fridge with a crochet cover and a showpiece on top; a rotary then push-button phone (with a long waiting list for a connection) under a doily; a calendar from the kirana shop; the pressure-cooker whistle count; kerosene stoves and LPG cylinder bookings.
- **Daily rhythm:**
  - 5:30 milk packets; 6:00 newspaper and *muggulu*.
  - 7:30 the school auto honks.
  - 1:00 tiffin box; 4:00 home, Boost or Horlicks, then play.
  - "**Ghar aao before the street lights come on.**"
  - 7:00 homework; 8:00 TV; 9:00 news (parents' remote time).
- **Doordarshan Sunday:**
  - 8 am *Rangoli* [V].
  - Late morning: cartoons, then *Shaktimaan* from 1997 [V]. Kids sprint home and the streets empty.
  - Afternoon regional film.
  - Evening Hindi film [~], followed by power cuts during the climax.
- **Power cuts ("current poyindi!"):** candles, emergency lights, hand fans, everyone on the terrace, ghost stories, the antakshari game (no lyrics in-game; just "your turn, song starting with *ma*!").
- **Ration shop (PDS):** queues with a ration card for rice, sugar and kerosene, with kids sent to hold the place in line.
- **Newspapers:** *Eenadu*, *Andhra Jyothi*, *Andhra Bhoomi* and *Vaartha* (Telugu); *Deccan Chronicle*, *The Hindu*, *Indian Express* and *Newstime* (unverified) (English); *Siasat*, *Munsif* and *Rahnuma-e-Deccan* (Urdu).
- **STD/ISD/PCO booths:** yellow signboards and glass cabins with a pulse-meter display. Cheaper night rates drew late-night queues to call relatives in the Gulf or the US (exact slab times unverified). They double as the game's **save points**.
- **VCR culture:** renting a VCR plus three cassettes for a night was a family event, with VCDs from 1998. Video libraries have handwritten catalogues.
- **The "Mummy chappal" meme:** the slipper as the universal discipline tool, thrown with legendary accuracy; the kid dodges it. It is the in-game **Chappal Meter**: getting caught leads to scolding, not harm. Related lines: "Ruk, aane de Abba ku!" (Wait till your father comes!) and "Chappal kidhar hai meri?!"
